# CP4 - Customer Golden Signals and Metrics Catalog

## A. Introduction

The critical journey is customer registration followed by customer list refresh. This catalog connects [CP1's component and trace map](01-journey.md), [CP2's JSON contract](02-log-contract.md) and [CP3's executable instrumentation](03-instrumentation/HOW-TO-RUN.md).
`ClienteForm`, `Clientes`, `clienteService` and `mockClientes` are components of the browser application, not independently deployed backend services. CP3 is a standalone laboratory using simulated HTTP transport; its metrics describe adapter attempts, not measured production registrations or localStorage writes.
Implemented means generated by CP3 code in memory and exposed as Prometheus-format text. It does not mean scraped or deployed. All PromQL below is proposed for future integration and has not been executed against a Prometheus server; no query results or production values are claimed.

## B. Four Golden Signals

| Signal | Meaning for Customer | Metric and coverage | Behavior suggesting a problem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Latency | Time to create a customer and refresh the visible list; slow refresh can leave the operator uncertain after saving. | Implemented `customer_operation_duration_seconds` measures each adapter attempt. Proposed `customer_journey_duration_seconds` covers submit through render or terminal failure. | Increasing mean or p95 relative to a validated baseline, especially slow refresh after a successful save. No production baseline exists here. |
| Traffic | Volume of customer creation and list-refresh attempts, needed to interpret load and failures. | Implemented `customer_operations_total` distinguishes `create` and `refresh`; proposed `customer_journeys_total` counts complete interactions once. | Unexpected traffic changes or diverging create/refresh rates under comparable usage. Not every refresh is necessarily paired with a create. |
| Errors | Failed adapter attempts, rejected forms, and partial journeys where saving succeeds but refresh fails. | Implemented counter filtered by `outcome="failure"`; proposed journey and validation-rejection counters cover missing browser-level outcomes. | Rising failure fraction, repeated refresh failures, or increased validation rejections. Rejections are distinct from transport failures. |
| Saturation | Pressure from pending operations relative to a configured concurrency budget; asynchronous waits can accumulate even in a browser. | Proposed `customer_operations_in_flight` and `customer_operation_concurrency_limit`; no current saturation measurement. | Sustained concurrency approaching/exceeding its configured positive budget, particularly with rising latency. This is a proposed pressure proxy, not measured CPU utilization. |

## C. Metrics Catalog

Status is scoped to this laboratory. All additional names below are explicitly proposed; CP4 changes no instrumentation code.

| Name | Type | Unit | Description / observation boundary | Allowed labels | Signal | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `customer_operations_total` | counter | operations | Increment once in `customerHttp` finally, for a successful response, non-2xx response or transport exception. | `operation`: `create`, `refresh`; `outcome`: `success`, `failure` | Traffic, errors | Implemented in CP3; Prometheus integration pending |
| `customer_operation_duration_seconds` | histogram | seconds | Adapter elapsed time measured using `performance.now()`; includes successful and failed attempts. | No application labels; generated `_bucket` series has `le` | Latency | Implemented in CP3; Prometheus integration pending |
| `customer_journeys_total` | counter | journeys | Count CP1 root R exactly once at its terminal outcome, including a failed post-save refresh. | `outcome`: `success`, `failure`, `partial_failure`, `rejected` | Traffic, errors | Proposed |
| `customer_journey_duration_seconds` | histogram | seconds | CP1 root R duration from submission through refreshed render, or terminal failure/rejection; not a sum of overlapping spans. | None; generated bucket label `le` | Latency | Proposed; initially reuse CP3 bucket boundaries |
| `customer_validation_rejections_total` | counter | rejections | Count form validation failures before `onGuardar`; do not treat them as HTTP failures. | `reason`: `missing_required_field`, `invalid_email` | Errors | Proposed |
| `customer_operations_in_flight` | gauge | operations | Increment before an adapter attempt, decrement in finally; aggregate pending work per application instance. | `operation`: `create`, `refresh` | Saturation | Proposed |
| `customer_operation_concurrency_limit` | gauge | operations | Positive configured concurrency budget for the same instance and operation; not an existing enforced backend limit. | `operation`: `create`, `refresh` | Saturation | Proposed; budget must be established before use |

### Histogram buckets and observation semantics

CP3 has cumulative `le` boundaries `0.005`, `0.05`, `0.5`, `5`, `+Inf` seconds, plus `_sum` and `_count`. Every observation increments all applicable buckets. These are classic histogram series; the base family name is not a directly emitted scalar sample.
The boundaries distinguish short local simulations, quick operations, noticeable waits and multi-second delays. They are laboratory choices, not evidence of acceptable user response times. Validate them with store operators and measured create/refresh distributions before production use; consider additional boundaries near the agreed user-experience threshold. The large 0.5-to-5-second gap makes p95 estimates coarse, and values above 5 seconds fall only into +Inf.
The existing histogram combines both operations and outcomes; it cannot yield per-operation or success-only p95. Adding `operation` would require a future instrumentation change and a revised series budget, not merely a query edit.

## D. Proposed PromQL Queries

These expressions assume continuous collection of the documented families, sufficient samples in each five-minute window, and selectors scoped to one comparable Customer deployment. Future scrape labels such as `job` must be configured before deployment-specific selectors can be supplied. Apply `rate` before aggregation to account for counter resets.

### 1. Mean adapter latency in seconds

```promql
sum(rate(customer_operation_duration_seconds_sum[5m])) / sum(rate(customer_operation_duration_seconds_count[5m]))
```

This is an operation-weighted mean across create and refresh, including failures. It is not end-to-end journey latency. For the proposed journey histogram, use the same expression with `customer_journey_duration_seconds_sum` and `_count`.

### 2. Adapter p95 latency in seconds

```promql
histogram_quantile(0.95, sum by (le) (rate(customer_operation_duration_seconds_bucket[5m])))
```

Keep `le` during aggregation. This estimates p95 for the classic histogram; the proposed journey histogram would use `customer_journey_duration_seconds_bucket` instead. Both require actual histogram observations and scraping.

### 3. Traffic in completed adapter operations per second

```promql
sum by (operation) (rate(customer_operations_total[5m]))
```

Multiply this expression by 60 for operations per minute. Since the counter increments in finally, it counts completed attempts rather than arrivals; pending work requires the proposed in-flight gauge.

### 4. Adapter failure percentage

```promql
100 * ((sum(rate(customer_operations_total{outcome="failure"}[5m])) or vector(0)) / sum(rate(customer_operations_total[5m])))
```

The numerator counts failed attempts, including transport exceptions and non-2xx responses. `or vector(0)` handles a missing failure series when successful observations exist; it cannot establish exporter health. No traffic means an undefined fraction, not proof of zero errors. A missing or insufficiently sampled denominator also prevents interpretation. CP3 emits only observed counter combinations, not preinitialized zero series.
For the proposed journey counter, failures should include `failure` and `partial_failure`; validation `rejected` outcomes should be reported separately. A successful save followed by failed refresh is one partially failed journey, not two failed journeys just because CP2 contains two ERROR log records.

### 5. Concurrency pressure ratio, proposed gauges only

```promql
max by (operation) (customer_operations_in_flight / customer_operation_concurrency_limit)
```

The gauges must have matching instance/operation label sets and strictly positive configured limits. Divide per instance before taking the maximum by operation. A ratio approaching one indicates pressure against the proposed budget; no CPU, thread-pool, queue, database or broker utilization is inferred. This query has no current CP3 data source.

### 6. Validation rejections per second, proposed counter only

```promql
sum by (reason) (rate(customer_validation_rejections_total[5m]))
```

Keep validation rejection rates separate from adapter error percentages: invalid forms never reach the adapter.

## E. Label Cardinality and Privacy

| Family | Bounded values and maximum series per application instance |
| --- | --- |
| Implemented operations counter | 2 operations x 2 outcomes = 4; only observed combinations currently appear. |
| Implemented adapter histogram | 5 `le` buckets + sum + count = 7; no operation/outcome labels. |
| Proposed journey counter | 4 terminal outcomes = 4. |
| Proposed journey histogram | Reusing the 5 boundaries gives 7 series; no application labels. |
| Proposed rejection counter | 2 controlled reasons = 2. |
| Each proposed concurrency gauge | 2 operations = 2; both gauges together contribute 4. |

The implemented families have at most 11 series per instance; the proposed additions contribute 17, for 28 total if all are implemented as specified. Scrape instances and deployment labels multiply this budget. Every new label needs an explicit finite value set and a recalculated budget.
Do not label metrics with customer names, email addresses, phone numbers, addresses, customer IDs, `trace_id`, `span_id`, correlation IDs, raw URLs, exception messages or request/response bodies. Do not hash personal data into labels. Aggregate outcomes and fixed operations are sufficient; trace identifiers belong only in the controlled CP2 log/tracing context.
CP3 currently permits only `operation` and `outcome` on its counter and generated `le` on histogram buckets. It does not emit `data_mode`, `component` or `service` metric labels. Do not filter by those labels as if present; any future mode separation must be explicit and bounded.

## G. Current Limitations

CP3 generates an in-memory counter and classic histogram and exposes text via `CustomerMetrics.prometheus()`, printed by its demo. There is no demonstrated scrape endpoint, Prometheus deployment, dashboard, production time series or integration into the Customer Portal. A short demo run cannot establish a usable five-minute rate history.
The actual portal's MOCK storage work, validation, render completion and detached refresh are not measured by the standalone adapter. Rejections, journey-level outcomes and saturation gauges remain proposed. Collector lifetime, zero-series initialization, deployment isolation and safe export must be designed before continuous monitoring; browser sessions cannot be treated as stable scrape targets without a justified collection architecture.
No functional Customer backend, database, RabbitMQ integration, infrastructure utilization or production performance is invented. CP4 defines this catalog only; it does not implement future metrics, SLOs, alerting or another checkpoint. The student's personal reflection remains separate.
